HALO MUSIC: GOVERNED, AUDITED, REVERSIBLE WORKFLOW
Specification v1 | Status: proposed architecture + offline executable contract

1. SCOPE AND HONEST SAFETY CLAIM

Protect artist masters, rights/consent, private metadata, payments, catalog
publication and playback continuity. "No unfixed faults" means no invisible,
unowned or falsely closed faults; it cannot mean zero defects, zero data loss
or instantaneous failover under every partition. Containment is not a fix.
Availability never justifies publishing without rights or discarding a master.

This PR does NOT enable a production council, autonomous repairs, redundant
storage, a new API, or a deployment controller. Existing endpoints retain their
behavior. netlify/lib/music-safety-contract.mjs is a pure transition planner;
scripts/music-safety-contracts.mjs exercises it with synthetic attestations.
Run npm run test:music-safety; npm test also runs it through posttest.
Do not import the planner into a mutating endpoint until section 10 is met.

2. EXISTING SYSTEM AND BOUNDARY

HALO_AI_COMMITTEE_WORKFLOW.md defines Builder, Verifier and Committee evidence
roles. Retain its PR review and human acceptance process.
services/djCouncilEngine.js is a deterministic DJ mix quality gate, not an
authorization service or a general-purpose governance quorum.
netlify/functions/maintenance-issues.mjs and
netlify/lib/maintenance-issues-validation.mjs track maintenance status.
The latter permits healed with verificationUnavailableReason: that is NOT
sufficient for this specification's verified/closed states.
netlify/lib/halo-ledger.mjs and netlify/functions/halo-ledger.mjs are existing
ledger integration points, not proof of atomic governance audit persistence.
netlify/functions/unified-upload.mjs and netlify/functions/upload-pipeline.mjs
are upload integration boundaries; release-conveyor functions and
netlify/lib/release-conveyor-service.mjs are release orchestration boundaries.
netlify/lib/release-conveyor.mjs already has a single-song councilDecision gate;
the conveyor's leases and events should be reused, not confused with independent
multi-brain authorization or a cross-system audit transaction.
Future adapters reference existing song, upload and issue IDs; do not create a
competing catalog, rewrite upload states or replace existing DJ quality gates.

3. LAYERS, AUTHORITY AND TRUST

Artist/request -> domain recommendations -> council -> safety arbiter
              -> transactional orchestrator/outbox -> bounded execution adapter
              -> independent verification -> audit/repair reconciliation
Human operators may freeze/isolate/restore through a separate audited channel.

Local domain brains:
- Ingestion/storage: resumability, checksums, replica lag and durable completion.
- Audio: format/preflight, derivative quality, loudness and playback health.
- Catalog/rights: metadata consistency, territory/consent, publication eligibility.
- Delivery/commerce: availability, entitlement and settlement consistency.
- Support/operations: artist communication, incident severity and ownership.
Brains receive least-privilege read snapshots and produce proposals, never
storage credentials, direct writes, deployment permissions or final approval.
Uploaded text, tags and model output are untrusted data, not instructions.

Council: three registered, separately authenticated seats using distinct paths:
deterministic domain rules, adversarial reasoning, and independent replay/runtime
validation. Different prompts to the same model are not sufficient diversity.
For v1 all THREE must approve with evidence: 3-of-3 quorum. Missing response,
abstention, veto, stale evidence or disagreement blocks progression, even if
two approve. The fix owner cannot vote. Confidence is advisory, not authority.
Conflicts produce a decision record naming disputed assumptions, requested
experiments and a deadline. Owner revises proposal, independent validators rerun,
then council votes again; no loop may silently weaken requirements.

Top-level safety arbiter: deterministic policy enforcement outside the reasoning
models; separate identity from owner and all council seats. Checks rights,
blast radius, approvals, rollback, compatibility, audit and freeze status.
It may deny or escalate, never invent evidence or bypass a failed council.
High/critical risk also requires an authenticated human release approval distinct
from the council and arbiter. Unknown risk is classified high until reviewed.

Audit & repair: durable issue register, evidence store, append-only decision log,
deadline sweeper, drift detection and reconciliation. At least one independent
monitor uses raw storage/queue/catalog observations rather than brain summaries.
Audit writers cannot erase history; a repair creates a new event.

Human override: scoped freeze, isolate a brain, stop promotion, revoke execution
leases and restore known-good state. Require identity, reason, ticket, scope,
expiry and notification. Two human operators approve destructive recovery,
rights/payment changes or unfreezing a high-risk incident. Override is never an
unlogged policy bypass: consent, evidence and audit remain mandatory. Emergency
containment can stop writes immediately; durable local emergency logging and
operator notification must exist when the central audit store is unavailable.
Reconcile that log before restart. The starter planner intentionally implements
no override, freeze writer, emergency logger or recovery transition.

4. ISSUE AND CHANGE LIFECYCLE

Every incident AND upgrade follows these normal forward states. Each transition
has actor, time, expected revision, request ID, proposal digest and audit event.
Blocked work remains in its current state with a reason and escalation timer;
blocked/contained is never treated as closed.

State        Entry evidence / accountable role / exit condition
detected     Durable signal + correlation ID; operations on-call is initial owner.
classified   Domain + severity + affected artists/assets/territories; triage owner.
assigned     Named accountable principal, backup rotation, deadline and acknowledgement.
planned      Immutable proposal: artifact, policy, environment, target scope,
             rollback procedure and independent verification procedure; fix owner.
simulated    Sandbox/replay results, mixed-version compatibility and successful
             rollback rehearsal on representative data; validation team.
reviewed     Three independent approvals, arbiter permit, high-risk human approval;
             unresolved dissent blocks; release manager owns the decision.
limited      Shadow (no writes or public output) or bounded canary; release operator
             uses a scoped execution lease and a predeclared observation window.
verified     Independent verifier, not fix owner, confirms limited health and
             expected behavior against the proposal; no "verification unavailable".
expanded     Controlled promotion through predeclared cohorts; release operator.
closed       Expanded observation AND catalog/rights/replica reconciliation pass,
             artist impact resolved, evidence retained; issue owner + verifier.

Detection must atomically assign the on-call owner; if routing fails use the
incident commander fallback. Provisional classification/owner at detection avoids
an unowned interval. A later reassignment is acknowledged and audited, never
clears ownership. Initial response targets (operational policy, not claims about
current automation): critical page immediately, acknowledge within 5 minutes,
contain within 15 minutes; high acknowledge within 30 minutes, plan within 4 hours;
medium/low acknowledge within one business day, agree a dated remediation plan.
Missed deadline pages backup then incident commander after 5 further minutes.
Overdue issues stay open and visible; no automatic downgrade or risk acceptance
closes a critical issue. Report every unresolved fault, next check and owner.

Failures at any stage record evidence, stop forward promotion and open/retain a
repair incident. Rollback/containment are separate audited emergency commands,
NOT backwards normal transitions or a shortcut to closed. After rollback start a
new proposal attempt linked to the incident; old evidence and decisions persist.
Any material proposal change invalidates old attestations and requires a new
digest and re-review. Production revision/attempt handling is an adapter task.

5. GUARANTEES AND ENFORCEMENT POINTS

No critical issue unowned: mandatory owner/deadline on every planner transition;
production detection transaction, on-call fallback and ownership sweeper required.
No high-risk deploy without approval: council + independent arbiter + human proof
bound to the exact proposal; missing/expired proof fails closed.
No silent override: separate audited human command with expiry and notification;
starter has no bypass flag or override execution.
No rollout without rollback: plan plus successful rehearsal required before
simulation/review and every later transition.
No fix without verification: limited independent verification before expansion;
expanded observation and reconciliation before closure.
No state change without audit: planner requires audit readiness and returns an
audit INTENT only; production transaction must commit state + append-only event +
outbox together, or none. A log call after a write is insufficient.
No partial upgrade without compatibility: attestations and runtime version
fencing required; incompatibility pauses consumers, not silently drops jobs.

6. INTERFACES AND ORCHESTRATION CONTRACT

All remote messages: schemaVersion, requestId/idempotencyKey, correlationId,
issueId, proposalDigest, policyVersion, sender identity, timestamp, expiry.
Authenticate the transport and producer. Reject unknown schemas, oversized input,
replayed requests, stale snapshots and unauthorized roles server-side.

DomainSnapshot -> Recommendation:
  input: immutable snapshot refs/checksums, domain, supported versions, scope.
  output: brain/version, recommendation, risk, confidence, evidence refs,
          assumptions, dissent, validationPath, proposed bounded actions.
CouncilRequest -> CouncilDecision:
  input: immutable proposal + authenticated recommendations + registered seats.
  output: per-seat vote/evidence, dissent, decision rationale, expiry.
SafetyRequest -> Permit/Denial:
  input: proposal, council decision, policy, live freeze/audit/rights state.
  output: scoped single-use permit or reasons/escalation; no direct execution.
Execute -> ExecutionReceipt:
  input: permit, expected revision, idempotency key, bounded cohort, deadline.
  output: job/attempt IDs, side-effect refs, checksums, observed version, failures.
Verify -> Attestation:
  input: independent observations + expected acceptance thresholds.
  output: passed, actorId, evidenceRef, proposalDigest, checkedAt, expiresAt.
AuditAppend -> durable event sequence:
  actor, role, issue, from/to, revision, proposal/policy/artifact versions,
  evidence refs, votes/dissent/rationale, scope, override and rollback references.

Starter API: planMusicSafetyTransition({
  record: { id, state, revision, severity, owner, dueAt, proposalDigest,
            plan: { rollbackRef, verificationRef, mode: "shadow" | "canary" } },
  to, expectedRevision, actorId, requestId, now,
  checks: { auditReady, simulation, compatibility, rollback, votes, safety,
            human, frozen, limitedHealth, verification, expandedHealth,
            reconciliation }
}) -> { ok: false, errors } OR { ok: true, auditIntent }.
All proof objects use the Verify attestation shape; votes additionally carry a
validationPath. now comes from the trusted server clock, not a caller timestamp.
Digest is SHA-256 of canonical immutable proposal content including issue ID,
artifact checksum, plan, severity, policy version, environment and rollout scope.
Proof references identify retained immutable reports, not arbitrary URLs to fetch.

The planner checks shape, binding, freshness, identity separation and stage gates.
It does NOT verify signatures, council registration, truth of passed booleans,
actual diversity, report content, hash canonicalization, RBAC, audit durability,
replay protection, database locking or actual rollback/observability thresholds.
Never accept checks from a browser, uploaded metadata or unauthenticated agent.
Adapters must load an authoritative record, resolve identities against a fixed
registry, validate evidence and assemble checks server-side. Retain full signed
attestations alongside the intent's evidence refs.

In one database transaction: lock/CAS expected revision; enforce requestId
uniqueness; validate permit and fresh authoritative proofs; append audit event;
update state/revision; insert execution outbox item. Outbox consumers use fenced
leases and idempotent effects, never assume exactly-once network delivery.
On uncertain outcome query receipt and reconcile before retry. Dispatch only after
commit. Recheck freeze, scope, expiry and rights at execution, not only planning.
Audit outage denies new mutations and promotions; known-good read-only playback
may continue. No model or council can grant itself additional permissions.

7. UPGRADE, ROLLOUT AND RECOVERY RUNBOOK

Release plan must declare cohort IDs, max assets/jobs, error budget, observation
duration, minimum samples, stop thresholds, owner and tested rollback artifact.
Default pilot for reversible internal changes: shadow for at least 24h and 100
representative jobs, then canary <=1% of eligible internal jobs for 30 minutes and
100 jobs, then <=10%, <=50%, 100%, each with equivalent health gates. Low traffic
waits or adds representative replay; it does not waive evidence. Rights, money,
master deletion or irreversible changes require separately approved manual plans;
never randomly expose artists to unsafe experiments.

Before shadow: pin policy/model/artifact versions; test current and previous
producer/consumer schemas, feature flags, queued job replay, database backups,
restore and regional failover. Upgrade one brain/failure domain at a time.
Use expand/contract migrations: additive fields first, dual-readable old/new jobs,
old readers retained until queues drain and rollback window closes. Fence unknown
job versions into a visible owned quarantine. Never roll all three seats and
arbiter together. Keep previous known-good binaries and policy available.

Shadow has no publish, payment, notification or storage mutation capability.
Compare candidate and baseline using independent deterministic rights/checksum
rules and replay fixtures; disagreement is an incident, not a vote to ignore it.
Canary uses explicit allowlisted scope, bounded queue concurrency and kill switch.
Expand only after independent health evidence. Critical rights/consent leak,
master checksum mismatch, duplicate charge or audit loss: immediate stop, page
and isolate. Example availability stop: error rate >1% OR >2x baseline for 5
minutes, or p95 latency >2x baseline for 5 minutes. Insufficient telemetry blocks
promotion. Product/SRE must approve service-specific thresholds before production.

Recovery: freeze writes/promotions; preserve receipts and evidence; isolate bad
worker; restore known-good artifact/config; drain or quarantine incompatible jobs;
verify checksums/rights and replay only idempotent work; independently reconcile
catalog and replicas; inform affected artists; two-person approval to unfreeze.
Do not "rollback" irreversible artist exposure or payments by deleting evidence:
use explicit compensation, notify rights/finance owners and keep incident open.
Exercise restore and failover quarterly and before risky infrastructure upgrades.
Set and demonstrate per-service RPO/RTO before launch; do not promise subsecond
failover or zero loss without measured durability and disaster-recovery evidence.

Music upload safety: retain the original until durable checksum-verified
completion; resume by upload/session + chunk identity; verify full-object hashes.
Replica acknowledgement is separate from local/network success. If a required
replica is unavailable, retain the source, queue bounded retry and show pending
durability; do not publish or claim redundancy. Derivative jobs consume immutable
object/version references only after completion. No split-stream success claim
can replace durable acknowledgements. Workers use idempotency, bounded backoff,
leases, poison-job quarantine and an owned dead-letter queue.

8. OBSERVABILITY AND COLLECTIVE FAILURE

Measure upload completion/checksum errors, replica lag, queue age/retries/DLQ,
catalog divergence, rights violations, playback success/latency, audit/outbox lag,
quorum availability/dissent, approval age, open issues/overdue ownership and
rollback success. Correlate request -> upload -> job -> song -> decision -> release.
Separate restricted decision evidence from redacted operational metrics; never
log raw audio, credentials, payment data or private artist metadata. Proposed
retention: redacted operational logs 30 days; restricted governance evidence
365 days, subject to legal/privacy review, access control and deletion policy.

Page on any safety invariant violation or unowned critical signal. Alert owner on
threshold drift, unexpected agreement/behavior changes or missing telemetry for
5 minutes; freeze promotions if audit heartbeat or quorum is unavailable.
Use an out-of-band monitor and manual drill so an unhealthy council cannot report
itself healthy. Diverse data sources, deterministic checks, adversarial fixtures,
independent humans and separate provider/failure domains mitigate common-mode
errors; identical model votes are not independent proof. A single compromised
reviewer cannot write, suppress dissent or override a safety veto.

Artist-facing status distinguishes receiving, retrying, pending durability,
processing, quarantined and complete. Notify on material incident, planned upgrade,
containment, rollback and verified resolution; include impact/next update, never
private decision evidence. Upgrade notification does not substitute for consent.

9. ENGINEERING HANDOFF (SEPARATE REVIEWED WORK PACKAGES)

Accountable roles below must be assigned to named maintainers before work starts.
Builder/Verifier/Committee remain separate as in the existing committee workflow.
No agent receives deployment authority merely by accepting a work package.

A. Operations + data owner: issue register, on-call fallback, deadlines and audit
   transaction/outbox. Accept when crash injection proves no unaudited state,
   replay is idempotent, and every critical issue has acknowledged ownership.
B. Security/policy owner: authenticated registry, proposal hashing, RBAC, evidence
   retention/signatures, safety arbiter, freeze/override commands. Accept when
   forged/expired proofs, duplicate identities and silent overrides are denied.
C. Ingestion/release owner: shadow-only adapter around existing pipeline, then
   bounded canary/rollback. Accept when worker crash, network loss, duplicate
   delivery, corrupt chunks and unavailable replica cannot publish unsafe output.
D. Independent verification/SRE owner: dashboards, chaos/replay, reconciliation,
   mixed-version and restore drills. Accept when quorum loss, correlated-model
   error, partial upgrade, audit outage and rollback failure stop promotion and
   page a human with evidence. Verify canary and expanded windows independently.

Each handoff PR includes scope/non-goals, owner/deadline, fixtures, reproducible
commands, rollback, Builder evidence, independent Verifier findings and Committee
decision using .github/pull_request_template.md. Send-back retains ownership.
Implement A/B before enabling C; D validates each stage. No autonomous repair
until shadow results and an explicit human production-readiness review pass.

[ ] Atomic issue/audit/outbox persistence, evidence access controls and retention.
[ ] Authenticated fixed council, independent arbiter, RBAC and replay protection.
[ ] Human approval, emergency logging, freeze/isolation and dual-control recovery.
[ ] Version compatibility, fenced jobs, real rollback and tested restore evidence.
[ ] Independent telemetry, service thresholds, representative shadow/canary data.
[ ] Existing upload/release adapters tested for bounded idempotent side effects.
[ ] Artist communications, rights/privacy review, RPO/RTO and human acceptance.

Until all are proven, this is a specification and offline contract, not a claim
that the live Halo Music platform already enforces these safety guarantees.
